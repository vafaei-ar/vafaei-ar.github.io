About

From physics to clinical AI

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Public Health Sciences at Penn State College of Medicine. I trained as a physicist and now work on applied machine learning in healthcare.

I collaborate with clinicians and data scientists to study what happens when models meet incomplete records, differences between hospitals, and practical constraints in patient care. My interests also include uncertainty, reproducible research, and the resources needed to run AI systems.

01Environmental impact of AIThe Lancet Digital Health · 2024

How much carbon might AI generate in pathology?

Our modeling study examined the carbon footprint of AI-supported pathology. We compared scenarios for model size, tissue selection, and computational strategies.

One tissue-analysis comparison found a much smaller modeled footprint for pathomics than for multitask deep learning. This result applies to the study's specific comparison, not every clinical AI task.

02Missing data in health recordsScientific Reports · 2025

One imputation method does not fit every variable.

Electronic health records contain substantial missing data. I developed Pympute to compare imputation methods and select models based on their performance on withheld values.

On the published MIMIC benchmark, the Flexible approach achieved the lowest reported holdout MAPE and RMSE among the methods evaluated. The chart uses the actual aggregate results from Table 1.

03Clinical prediction · Bioengineering 2025

Can ECG and EHR data better identify hidden atrial fibrillation?

We combined ECG waveforms and clinical variables in a pilot cohort of 189 stroke patients, including 49 with paroxysmal AF. The published performance is exploratory and requires external validation.

04Computational pathology · Nature Communications 2023

Turning tissue images into measurable biological information

In collaborative work on the FLASH framework, we analyzed 1,043 cases. Segmentation and morphometry supported quantitative study of kidney tissue structures and clinical associations.

The original study includes real histopathology images and segmentation examples. This summary displays its reported aggregate counts.
